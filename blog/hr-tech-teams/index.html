Why HR and tech teams misunderstand each other

I have lived on both sides of this. I studied computer engineering, then worked in SEO and content, then worked as an HR Business Partner in a small dental clinic. The same meeting can sound completely different depending on which side of the table you sit on.

It is rarely about bad intentions. It is about language. A developer says “this will take two weeks because the database needs restructuring” and hears a clear, honest answer. An HR manager hears “no, and I will not explain why in a way I can repeat to the team”. Both are frustrated, and neither is wrong.

Three small habits that help

1. Translate the “why”, not just the “what”. “We need to delay the launch” is a decision. “We need to delay the launch because the payment page fails for 1 in 20 users, and fixing it now is cheaper than losing those customers” is a decision people can support.

2. Agree on one number. Teams argue less when they share a measure — time to hire, time to fix a bug, number of candidates who finish onboarding. Pick one, write it down, and check it together.

3. Ask the question the other side is afraid to ask. In HR that is often “what does this actually mean for my day-to-day work?” In tech it is often “what happens if we do nothing?” Asking it out loud saves weeks of guessing.

My goal is to be the person in the middle — not to take sides, but to make sure both sides are actually talking about the same thing.

That is why I am studying Business Management now, and why I want to move into HR: I do not want to leave the technical world behind. I want to use it to help people work better together.
